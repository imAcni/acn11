# Journal Entry 1

Currently I am playing in the NJCCIC (New Jersey Cybersecurity and Communications Integrations Cell)'s Cybersecurity Competition. It is a good way to practice my skills and also to meet new people that are also fluent in cybersecurity in the state of New Jersey. With this competition, I hope to apply the skills and knowledge that i've previously developed to do well and qualify for the finals again, like last year. 

Alongside this, I also finished progress on the last `File Structure Exploits` module in my learning website, pwn.college. This module is part of the hardest learning module on the entire website, so I feel very accomplished after this. My next destination is to complete the Kernel Security Module, which is in a module that is one below the one that I completed. This one is different though, in programs, you usually have a `userland` binary, which interacts with processes on the user side, like regular programs. However, in the kernel side, this is the more lower level process on the computer, the part of the computer that is the lowest level and has a lot more permissions. I hope to complete this module by the end of March.

# Journal Entry 3
My expert was the first one to introduce me to the world of cybersecurity. They introduced me to CyberStart, which was a platform to easily learn cybersecurity and also turn it into a fun game. Through this platform, I thoroughly enjoyed cybersecurity, and have been addicted ever since. Without that inital push from my friend, I would have never been interested at all. We have drifted apart though, but I still appreciate them. 

This week was the most hectic by far. First, on the weekend, i had DiceCTF qualifiers, which is a competition based in New York, where the qualifiers would win a free trip to New York to compete in the finals. Obviously, my team and I weren't too experienced, so we couldn't stack up against the massive demand of competition, but we still placed a respectable 25th, which was about 10 slots away from qualifying. The real kicker of this week was PicoCTF, which is the hardest high school competition all year, hosted by Carnegie Mellon University. With the surge of AI, i've noticed many teams utilizing it to solve their problems. We placed in a respectable 7th place in the highschool division, which is not bad at all, considering every single top highschool team plays this CTF for the 4,000 dollar prize pool. We didn't win any cash prizes, but we were close to. I managed to solve the last, super difficult problem, which I will later document when the contest is over (It is still ongoing for another 2 weeks). I feel this is very significant to my journey, as it reflects clear progress in my skills.
